# What Screens Want

Created for Typography & Interaction (’26–’27) under the MPS Communication Design program at The New School—this project attempts to illustrate the essay “What Screens Want” by Frank Chimero through the form of an interactive website. It explores the relationship between typography, movement, and the way we interact with things on a screen.

# What Do Screens Want?

In this essay, Chimero questions what it actually means to design for screens. He argues that what makes screens special isn't necessarily how things look, but rather their ability to change. He describes this quality as “flux”—the idea that screens are constantly moving, responding, and transforming.

Unlike print, where everything stays in a fixed position, screens allow elements to behave differently depending on how we interact with them. In other words, designing for screens shouldn't just be about arranging things on a page, but also thinking about what those things can do.

# Inspiration Behind the Design

The idea of flux became a big inspiration behind the design of this project. If the ability to change is what makes screens different from print, then why should a website behave like a static page?

This question became the starting point for the project. Rather than simply placing the essay onto a webpage, I wanted to explore how the reading experience itself could reflect some of Chimero's ideas.

Through typography, movement, and interaction, the project attempts to make the text feel less like something fixed on a page and more like something that belongs on a screen. The idea was to let the design communicate parts of the essay through its behavior… rather than just through the words themselves.
